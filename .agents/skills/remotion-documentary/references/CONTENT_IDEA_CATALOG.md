# Content Idea Catalog

This file chooses **what the video is about**. It is separate from the visual style, character design, production workflow and Remotion implementation.

## Content pillars and example ideas

### A. History and historical events
- How did the Dutch build a country below sea level?
- How did the Great Wall of China change over time?
- Why did Rome build roads across its empire?
- How did people navigate before GPS?
- How did ancient cities get clean water?
- What caused a turning point in a major war?

### B. How things are made
- How is a modern bridge built across a wide river?
- How does a dam get built and hold back water?
- How are glass bottles manufactured?
- How is a semiconductor chip made?
- How are underground subway tunnels constructed?
- How is paper made from wood pulp?

### C. Inventions and discoveries
- How did the light bulb evolve from early experiments to practical electric lighting?
- How was the telephone invented?
- How did humans discover electricity?
- How did the first airplane become possible?
- How did the printing press change society?
- Who contributed to the light bulb, and why is the history more complex than one inventor?

### D. Infrastructure and hidden systems
- How does a city survive underground through sewers and drainage?
- How does an airport coordinate thousands of flights?
- How do undersea internet cables work?
- How do flood barriers protect a coastal city?
- How does a container port move cargo?
- How does a power grid balance electricity demand?

### E. Everyday objects and technology
- How does a refrigerator keep food cold?
- How does a microwave heat food?
- How does a touchscreen detect a finger?
- How does an elevator stay safe?
- How does a car differential work?
- How does a QR code store information?

### F. Science, nature and the planet
- Why do earthquakes happen?
- How do volcanoes form?
- Why does the Moon appear to change shape?
- How do migratory birds navigate?
- How do coral reefs support marine life?
- Why do some deserts turn green after rain?

### G. Civilizations, architecture and engineering
- How did ancient builders move massive stones?
- Why were medieval castles designed with layers of defence?
- How did skyscrapers become taller and safer?
- How do suspension bridges distribute forces?
- Why do some buildings need deep foundations?

### H. War, strategy and military history
- How did a particular battle unfold?
- How did logistics shape a historical campaign?
- How did radar change air defence?
- How did naval engineering evolve?
- How did codebreaking affect a historical conflict?

For sensitive historical topics, distinguish established facts, contested interpretations and speculation. Do not sensationalize victims or invent dialogue.

## Idea-selection procedure

1. Ask the user to choose a content pillar or say “surprise me”.
2. Generate 5–10 specific concepts in the chosen pillar.
3. For each concept show: working title, one-sentence premise, central question, visual potential, suggested story structure, research questions and likely factual pitfalls.
4. Ask the user to select one idea or combine compatible concepts.
5. Lock the viewer promise: “After watching, the viewer will understand…”
6. Research and fact-check before writing the final script.
7. Only after the topic is selected, ask about platform/duration and visual style.

## Score each idea 1–5

- Curiosity / hook
- Visual potential
- Beginning–middle–end story potential
- Credible source availability
- Fit to requested duration
- Clarity for the audience

## Story structures

- Mystery → reveal → explanation → consequence → takeaway
- Problem → failed attempts → breakthrough → modern result
- Raw material → process stages → finished object → real-world use
- Before → invention → adoption → impact
- Map/context → timeline → turning points → aftermath
- System overview → components → interactions → failure modes → resilience

## Research brief

Before scripting, record confirmed facts, dates, people, places, institutions, how the mechanism works, disputed accounts, primary/secondary sources, claims requiring citations, and what can/cannot be represented visually.

The script must not introduce factual claims absent from the research brief without flagging them for verification.

## Separation of responsibilities

- This file: topic and story premise.
- Visual Style Library: appearance and art direction.
- Character Model Guide: reusable characters and object model sheets.
- Production workflow: platform, timing, narration, scenes, assets and QA.
- Remotion project: implementation.

Do not let a character prompt invent facts or let a style guide write the story.
