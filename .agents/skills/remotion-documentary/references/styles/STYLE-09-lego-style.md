# STYLE-09 — Lego Style

## Reference reading
The references show a toy-brick miniature world: a red bus built from block-like bricks, green studded baseplate/ground, small minifigure-like people and simple brick-built city/office elements. The camera sees a physical toy diorama, not a generic voxel or Minecraft world.

## Visual signature
- Recognizable interlocking construction-brick language with visible studs.
- Minifigure-like characters with cylindrical heads, simple faces, block/hinge limbs and toy proportions.
- Brick-built vehicles, buildings, furniture and scenery.
- Bright saturated toy colors; the bus example uses red, green baseplate and yellow/blue accents.
- Plastic surface response with soft highlights and visible seams between bricks.
- Miniature tabletop/diorama scale.

## Geometry and materials
- Establish a consistent brick unit, stud spacing, plate thickness and minifigure scale.
- Build forms from plausible connected brick modules; avoid impossible floating assemblies.
- Vehicle body should read as a brick-built bus, not a smooth conventional bus with a toy texture applied.
- Use visible studs where the reference style calls for them; do not put studs on every surface indiscriminately.
- Use molded plastic with restrained gloss, rounded edges and subtle seams.

## Palette and lighting
- Sample the actual reference for exact brick colors. Use a saturated toy palette with a dominant hero color and supporting baseplate/environment colors.
- Lighting should resemble a well-lit miniature set: soft key light, gentle shadows and controlled plastic highlights.
- Maintain consistent toy scale and light direction.

## Camera and layout
- Use a miniature diorama camera, usually three-quarter view or slightly elevated tabletop angle.
- Keep minifigure, vehicle and environment scale coherent.
- Show enough ground/baseplate to establish the toy world.
- Use macro-like depth of field only moderately; the subject and important details must remain readable.

## Motion language
- Assemble the scene from bricks, move vehicles with wheel rotation, animate minifigure walk/arm gestures and reveal props in modular steps.
- Use slight stop-motion flavor only if deliberately selected; the reference itself should not be forced into choppy animation.
- Camera may gently dolly/orbit around the diorama.
- Preserve brick connections during all motion; do not deform bricks like soft clay.

## Asset and prompt requirements
Specify exact brick system proportions, stud visibility, minifigure scale, toy plastic finish, colors, camera angle and assembly state. Ask for original characters and generic construction-brick aesthetics rather than branded logos or exact protected sets. Separate assets when independently animated.

## Do not
Do not drift into voxel cubes, Minecraft blocks, generic plastic 3D, claymation, realistic humans, inconsistent stud dimensions or impossible brick connections.
