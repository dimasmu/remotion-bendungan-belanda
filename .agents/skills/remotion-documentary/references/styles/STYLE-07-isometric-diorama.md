## Isometric Diorama — STYLE-07

## Purpose and visual identity
A miniature world viewed from a consistent isometric or axonometric camera. Ideal for cities, factories, infrastructure networks and process visualization.

## Character construction
Characters are optional scale cues. If present, use compact simplified figures with consistent head/body ratio, clothing blocks and readable color coding. Their scale must remain consistent with buildings and machines. Avoid close-up facial realism.

## Geometry and camera
Lock isometric angle, orthographic camera, vertical axis, grid spacing and object scale. Parallel edges must follow the same projection rules. Never rotate the camera into perspective without a deliberate transition.

## Palette and materials
Use a cohesive miniature palette with exact HEX values. Materials may resemble painted wood, clay, paper or matte plastic; choose one dominant material language. Shadows should be soft and consistent.

## Environments and objects
Build modular terrain tiles, buildings, roads, pipes, water channels, machinery and landscape blocks. Cross-sections and underground layers should align to the same isometric grid. Label important components only when requested.

## Animation behavior
Use camera orbit only within a small approved range, object lifts, cutaway reveals, flowing paths and modular assembly. Maintain the isometric projection and grid alignment.

## Asset sheets
Create individual isometric asset sheets or orthographic component sheets for reusable modules. A character/object sheet should contain one subject or one coherent modular kit only. Asset count is driven by the storyboard.

## Prompt constraints
Specify camera azimuth/elevation or reference image, orthographic projection, grid, scale, material, palette and shadow. Exclude perspective drift, inconsistent scale and random decorative elements.
