## Clay / Stop-Motion — STYLE-09

## Purpose and visual identity
A handcrafted miniature world with tactile clay or sculpted materials, visible fingerprints/tool marks and deliberate frame-by-frame charm.

## Character construction
Characters, if needed, should look physically sculpted. Lock head/body proportions, eye placement, mouth construction, limb thickness, costume shapes and surface finish. Choose armature-like or fully clay limbs and keep the approach. Expressions should be achieved through controlled replacement parts or subtle sculpted changes.

## Surface and materials
Use matte clay, soft highlights, slight imperfections and consistent handmade texture. Fingerprints and seams are intentional but restrained. Avoid glossy plastic, polished metal everywhere or photoreal skin.

## Palette and lighting
Declare exact base colors and material variations. Use soft studio-like lighting, grounded contact shadows and consistent color temperature. Keep backgrounds and props in the same handcrafted material world unless intentionally contrasted.

## Environments and objects
Build miniature sets, props and machines with tactile scale cues. Moving parts should be physically understandable and remain consistent between frames.

## Camera and animation
Use locked-off or carefully controlled miniature cinematography. Motion is stepped and deliberate; specify frame cadence, holds, replacement poses and camera moves. Avoid smooth CG motion that breaks the stop-motion illusion.

## Asset sheets
Generate one character or object per sheet. Include only the turnarounds and replacement poses required by production. Keep lighting, scale and material consistent across the sheet.

## Prompt constraints
Specify clay type, surface finish, handmade imperfections, miniature scale, lighting and palette. Exclude glossy toy plastic, photorealism, unrelated props and changing character proportions.
