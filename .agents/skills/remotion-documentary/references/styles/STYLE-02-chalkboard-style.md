# STYLE-02 — Chalkboard Style

## Reference reading
Both references show a framed, upright green chalkboard with a dark green/blackboard surface and a simple white chalk drawing. One example explains a bus with a side elevation, wheels and small motion/diagram marks; the other shows an office scene with a person at a board. The board itself is the stage: the drawing is the content.

## Visual signature
- Deep forest-green or muted dark-green chalkboard, subtly dusty and slightly uneven.
- Warm wood or muted brown frame visible around the board in the reference.
- White chalk is dominant; a few restrained colored chalk marks may appear.
- Thin, hand-drawn outlines with imperfect pressure, chalk dust and broken stroke texture.
- Simple educational diagrams: outlines, arrows, underlines, wheels, motion marks, labels and small explanatory sketches.
- Flat, front-facing board presentation rather than a realistic classroom establishing shot.

## Geometry and line treatment
- Draw objects as chalk contour sketches, not filled cartoon illustrations.
- Use a consistent chalk stroke width with slight natural variation and grain.
- Keep line intersections legible; avoid overly dense hatching.
- Use simple perspective and diagrammatic simplification. A bus can be an outline with wheels and a few structural details rather than a fully shaded vehicle.
- If a human appears, use a simple chalk figure matching the same chalk stroke language.

## Palette and surface
- Sample board and chalk colors from the provided frame before setting HEX values.
- Base: dark green chalkboard; primary mark: warm white/off-white chalk; optional accent chalk: muted yellow, pale blue or soft pink.
- Add subtle chalk smudges, erased ghost marks and dust, but keep the diagram readable.
- The wooden frame is a secondary warm brown accent, not the dominant color.

## Camera and layout
- Use a mostly straight-on view with the board centered and its full frame visible when presenting a complete explanation.
- Use tighter crops for a single diagram detail, but preserve the recognizable board surface.
- Leave blank board space around the main drawing for labels added later in Remotion.
- Do not tilt or add dramatic perspective unless the scene explicitly calls for it.

## Motion language
- Reveal chalk strokes progressively along paths.
- Draw the outline first, then internal details, then arrows/labels/highlights.
- Use chalk wipe/erase transitions, underlines, pointer movements and subtle dust.
- A hand or chalk stick can enter frame only if the chosen shot requires it; the example does not require a visible hand in every scene.
- Keep motion deliberate and instructional, like a teacher building a diagram.

## Asset and prompt requirements
Specify dark green board, visible frame, white chalk line quality, exact diagram subject, straight-on framing, blank annotation space and whether ghost marks are allowed. Ask for no readable generated writing; put factual labels and typography in Remotion.

## Do not
Do not turn this into a whiteboard marker animation, glossy green UI panel, photorealistic classroom, colorful children's illustration, neon sketch or clean vector infographic.
