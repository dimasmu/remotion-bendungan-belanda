# Hand-Drawn Illustrated Documentary — STYLE-04

## Purpose and visual identity
An authored editorial illustration style for historical and human stories. It combines confident ink drawing, selective hatching and controlled colored-pencil/wash shading while preserving documentary clarity.

## Character construction
Characters use illustrated anatomy simplified to the required detail level. Lock silhouette, facial construction, proportion, clothing period, hair and distinguishing details. Expression and gesture should communicate narrative intent. Avoid changing drawing style between characters or shots.

## Linework and texture
Use deliberate outer contours, varied but controlled interior lines, hatching for shadow and sparse crosshatching for emphasis. Keep line weight hierarchy consistent. Paper grain and ink bleed are subtle, not noisy.

## Palette and rendering
Use a limited editorial palette with exact HEX swatches. Color may use flat washes, pencil texture or restrained watercolor-like fills, but choose one dominant treatment and document it. Shadows follow a consistent light direction.

## Environments and objects
Illustrate environments with perspective cues, architectural detail and selective texture. Important objects must preserve factual shape and component relationships. Use annotated detail insets only when useful.

## Layout and camera
Use illustrated wide shots, medium character shots and detail close-ups. Perspective may be expressive but must remain spatially coherent across connected shots.

## Animation behavior
Prefer multiplane parallax, drawn-on reveals, subtle line jitter, held poses and controlled camera moves. Avoid uncontrolled redraw flicker; maintain a canonical drawing reference for recurring assets.

## Asset sheets
Generate a separate sheet for each recurring character or important object. Include only needed views and details. For characters, use a single-character turnaround with optional expressions/poses. No group sheets unless explicitly requested as a crowd reference.

## Prompt constraints
Name the drawing medium, line hierarchy, hatching density, palette, paper tone and detail budget. Exclude photorealism, glossy 3D, inconsistent linework and unapproved costume changes.
