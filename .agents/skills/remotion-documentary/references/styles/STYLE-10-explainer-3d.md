# STYLE-10 — Explainer 3D

## Reference reading
The second reference shows a friendly stylized 3D human in a bright, minimal room/office. The character has simplified proportions, smooth surfaces, clean clothing and an approachable educational-animation appearance. The environment uses a few large furniture forms and soft pastel/neutral colors. Unlike CGI Motion, this is a bright, explanatory character-led world.

## Visual signature
- Friendly stylized 3D person with simplified but recognizable anatomy.
- Smooth, rounded geometry and clean surfaces.
- Bright, uncluttered office/room or educational environment.
- Soft neutral/pastel palette with a few colored props.
- Diffuse studio-like lighting, gentle shadows and clear separation.
- Character and environment share the same polished stylized render quality.

## Geometry and character design
- Define head/body ratio, limb thickness, hand/foot size, face detail, hair and clothing silhouette.
- Keep character anatomy stylized, not realistic; avoid changing proportions between shots.
- Use simple facial features and readable gestures.
- Furniture and objects are simplified into large clean forms with few small details.
- Use consistent bevel radius and smoothness across assets.

## Palette and lighting
- Sample colors from the reference before assigning HEX values. The shown direction is bright and soft: warm off-white/cream backgrounds, pale mint/teal or muted colored furniture, restrained clothing colors and gentle neutral shadows.
- Use a large soft key light, ambient fill and contact shadows.
- Avoid harsh dramatic contrast, strong rim glow or mirror-like surfaces.
- Keep the palette welcoming and readable.

## Camera and layout
- Use medium/full-body character shots, simple three-quarter room views and clean negative space.
- Camera is usually at character eye/chest height or a gentle elevated angle.
- Keep environment sparse so the character's action is the focus.
- Use subtle depth of field only if it does not hide important educational detail.

## Motion language
- Character walks, turns, points, holds or interacts with one clear object at a time.
- Use smooth, readable body mechanics with soft easing.
- Demonstrate processes in stages; support them with clean Remotion labels, icons or callouts.
- Camera movement is gentle tracking, dolly or small orbit, not dramatic cinematic motion.
- For recurring characters, maintain identical model, clothing, proportions and materials.

## Asset and prompt requirements
Specify character identity/proportions, pose, outfit, facial simplicity, room layout, prop count, pastel palette, soft lighting and camera. Use approved reference images for recurring characters. Keep text and labels editable in Remotion.

## Do not
Do not turn it into dark cinematic CGI, hyperreal humans, toy-brick Lego, flat vector 2D, anime, excessive glossy plastic or a cluttered detailed environment.
