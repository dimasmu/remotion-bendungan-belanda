## Stylized Cinematic 3D — STYLE-06

## Purpose and visual identity
A cohesive art-directed 3D world with cinematic lighting, appealing forms and controlled realism. It supports large infrastructure, historical reconstructions and emotionally legible scenes without aiming for strict photorealism.

## Character construction
Characters are optional. If needed, define body proportions, head size, facial topology, eye style, hair/clothing shapes, rig range and material response. Choose a stylization level and lock it. Avoid mixing character proportions or shader styles.

## Modeling and materials
Use clean silhouettes, intentional bevels and a consistent detail budget. Materials should be physically plausible but art-directed: roughness, metallic response, subsurface scattering and texture resolution must be specified. Avoid random surface detail.

## Palette and lighting
Define base colors in HEX plus material notes. Lock white balance, key/fill/rim direction, shadow softness, contrast and atmospheric treatment. Lighting continuity is part of the visual identity.

## Environments and objects
Use layered depth, coherent architecture and consistent scale. Hero objects require stable dimensions, component layout and material IDs. Historical reconstructions must separate sourced facts from artistic interpretation.

## Camera
Specify lens range, camera height, movement speed, depth of field and framing rules. Avoid arbitrary lens changes between adjacent shots.

## Animation behavior
Use rigged character animation, mechanical pivots, physics only when controlled, and smooth camera paths. Maintain temporal continuity of cloth, props and lighting.

## Asset sheets
Use one isolated character or object per reference generation. Turntables, expression poses and prop callouts are optional and chosen according to the shot plan. Production models/rigs are separate from reference sheets.

## Prompt constraints
Specify stylization level, topology/detail, materials, lighting rig, camera and approved palette. Exclude style mixing, uncontrolled realism and continuity-breaking redesigns.
