## Technical Blueprint / Infographic — STYLE-08

## Purpose and visual identity
A precise explanatory visual system using diagrams, cutaways, dimension lines, arrows, labels, exploded views and schematic geometry. It prioritizes correctness and legibility over character acting.

## Character construction
Characters are usually unnecessary. If a human scale figure is needed, use a minimal silhouette or standardized technical pictogram. Do not create expressive characters unless the narrative explicitly needs them.

## Line and diagram grammar
Define line weights for outlines, hidden edges, dimensions, leaders, flow paths and emphasis. Define arrowheads, node shapes, hatch patterns, section cuts and label hierarchy. Keep orthographic views and projection conventions consistent.

## Palette and typography
Use a limited technical palette with exact HEX values: canvas, primary line, secondary line, highlight, warning and muted annotation. Ensure high contrast and readable label sizes. Typography must be documented by role and hierarchy.

## Objects and environments
Prioritize accurate component relationships, flow direction, dimensions and interfaces. Use exploded assemblies, cross-sections, process diagrams, maps and schematic layouts. Every factual dimension or label must be source-checked.

## Camera and layout
Mostly orthographic, plan, elevation, section or axonometric views. Use precise alignment and consistent scale. Avoid cinematic perspective unless used as a clearly separated context shot.

## Animation behavior
Use line drawing, mask reveals, component separation, flow tracing, callout movement and step-by-step assembly. Every animated state must preserve logical connections.

## Asset sheets
Generate object/component sheets rather than character sheets unless human figures are required. One object or coherent assembly per sheet; include views/cutaways required by the explanation.

## Prompt constraints
Specify projection, line-weight hierarchy, annotation rules, exact palette, units and factual source requirements. Exclude decorative characters, ambiguous geometry, illegible labels and unsupported measurements.
