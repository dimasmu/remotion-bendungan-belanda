# STYLE-05 — Comic Art

## Reference reading
Both videos show a framed comic illustration. The bus example uses a blue halftone background, thick black outlines, a bright yellow jagged impact burst with a comic sound effect, a red bus, white smoke/cloud puffs and an expressive close-up character/speech bubble. The office example uses a comic panel with an illustrated worker and a speech bubble. The visual identity is panel-based graphic storytelling.

## Visual signature
- Strong black ink contours and clean, bold silhouette shapes.
- Flat saturated color fills with limited cel shading.
- Halftone/dotted screen texture in selected background areas.
- Comic panels, inset close-ups, speech balloons and jagged impact bursts.
- Exaggerated but readable facial expressions and poses.
- Sound-effect lettering and motion marks are optional story devices, not mandatory decoration.

## Geometry and line treatment
- Use a clear outline hierarchy: strong outer contour, thinner internal details.
- Characters have simplified expressive anatomy and readable gesture.
- Use graphic shadow shapes rather than soft realistic shading.
- Halftone is a controlled texture layer; do not cover every surface with dots.
- Speech bubbles and impact bursts must be intentionally placed and never obscure the focal subject.

## Palette and lighting
- Sample the actual comic frames for exact colors. The bus example uses a bright cyan/blue dotted field, yellow burst, red vehicle, white smoke and black outlines.
- Prefer a compact high-contrast palette with one dominant background color and a few saturated accents.
- Keep shadows graphic and hard-edged; avoid glossy 3D lighting.

## Camera and layout
- Compose as one clear comic panel or a purposeful sequence of panels.
- Use varied shot sizes: establishing panel, medium action panel, reaction close-up.
- Keep panel borders and speech balloons consistent.
- Reserve room for Remotion-added text if the image generator cannot render accurate wording.

## Motion language
- Use punch-in/punch-out, panel wipes, page turns, quick impact holds, speed lines and pop-in bursts.
- Animate speech balloons and captions in reading order.
- Use squash/stretch or brief impact shake sparingly.
- The timing should feel punchy and graphic, with short holds after important visual impacts.

## Asset and prompt requirements
Specify panel layout, subject pose/expression, ink thickness, halftone density, palette, speech-bubble content policy and action beat. Prefer blank speech bubbles; add exact dialogue and sound-effect typography in Remotion.

## Do not
Do not drift into anime, watercolor, photorealism, soft gradients, thin inconsistent outlines, excessive halftone noise or cluttered multi-panel layouts.
